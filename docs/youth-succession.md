# Youth Succession

Status: Institutional design direction; detailed governance and implementation are open. See the [README](../README.md) for the canonical overview.

## Engineer out founder dependency

Political Co-op should be designed from inception for long-term youth inheritance. Over a **minimum 20-year transition**, governance and operations should progressively move toward youth operation and control.

The purpose is institutional continuity: the service should survive the people who created it. A founder's undocumented knowledge, private credentials, or irreplaceable relationships are operational risks, not durable infrastructure.

Youth interns and contributors may be recruited from the beginning, but this is not required for initial launch. The continuity work can start immediately, regardless of who participates first.

## Begin with transferable operations

- Document systems, dependencies, decisions, and recurring workflows.
- Keep institutional records in version-controlled or otherwise auditable systems.
- Distribute operational knowledge; train replacements continuously.
- Document access grants, revocation, credential recovery, and emergency handoffs without exposing secrets.
- Practice handoffs so another person can operate and recover the service.
- Record succession responsibilities and make leadership turnover normal.

These practices support youth succession but also improve reliability today. Technical access must not automatically confer political or governance authority.

## Make the transition real, not ceremonial

Progress should include meaningful operational responsibility and governance control—not just internships alongside permanent founder vetoes. A minimum 20-year transition is a long-term design commitment, not a reason to defer documentation and training.

The start point, age ranges, participation model, governance mechanisms, milestones, and evidence of transferred control remain to be defined collaboratively. This repository does not prescribe seat quotas, a legal structure, or a completed transition schedule.

As participants age and new contributors join, the institution will need continuing recruitment and training rather than a single handoff to one cohort.

## Safeguards and sustainability

Before assigning youth responsibilities, resolve appropriate supervision, legal capacity, employment requirements, safeguarding, access to sensitive data, and accountable decision-making. These require qualified review where applicable; youth participation is not a shortcut around responsibilities.

Pair responsibility with training, realistic workloads, and support. The subscription model should support compensated work over time, not turn youth succession into a permanent unpaid staffing plan.

A practical continuity test: can a new, appropriately authorized operator understand the system, perform routine work, recover from failure, and train the next person without relying on the founder?
