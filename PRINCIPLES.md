# Principles

These are proposed design constraints, not a complete constitution or a claim that safeguards already exist. The [README](README.md) remains the canonical introduction.

## 1. Neutral infrastructure

Serve school-board candidates on equal terms regardless of party or affiliation. No party owns or receives preferential control of the infrastructure. Bootstrap participation does not confer political direction-setting rights.

Evaluate neutrality in access policies, service terms, data boundaries, and governance—not just branding. Document how conflicts of interest would be handled.

## 2. Earn the subscription

Offer a 90-day free trial and demonstrate enough value to earn an affordable monthly subscription. No final price is set. Do not use artificial lock-in to compensate for weak value.

## 3. Sustain the work

Recurring revenue should eventually support hosting, maintenance, development, security, customer support, and compensated workers. Volunteer participation is welcome; permanent dependence on unpaid essential labor is not the goal.

## 4. Establish information infrastructure at formation

Be CIO-first: version-controlled documentation, open standards, auditability, access governance, cybersecurity, credential recovery, workflow documentation, and institutional memory belong at the beginning.

Document recovery without publishing secrets. Separate public technical documentation from credentials and private campaign information. Test whether someone other than the original operator can follow the instructions.

## 5. Separate kinds of authority

Repository access, production credentials, and technical expertise must not automatically confer political or governance authority. Define permissions and decision rights explicitly as the institution forms.

## 6. Keep software inspectable and replaceable

Default to GPLv3 for software developed by the project. Document any practical reason for another license, including compatibility with reused components. Preserve third-party license obligations.

Prefer open standards, interoperability, portability, forkability, and public documentation. Define usable export and exit paths. Avoid unnecessary vendor lock-in; explain unavoidable dependencies.

## 7. Retain knowledge, protect people

Preserve reusable lessons and operational documentation, not unrestricted access to past campaigns' data. Plan for campaign separation, appropriate data retention, and applicable data-use requirements. AI-assisted workflows, if included, need human review and explicit data boundaries.

## 8. Make succession routine

Eliminate founder dependency and tribal knowledge. Train replacements, distribute operational knowledge, and document leadership handoffs. Design for progressive youth operation and control over a minimum 20-year transition; detailed milestones and safeguards remain open.

## 9. Reuse before rebuilding

Choose the MVP collaboratively. Investigate existing open-source projects before writing replacements. A feature list is a set of hypotheses, not a delivery promise.

## 10. Steward capability, not a frozen codebase

**Build once. Steward forever.** Patch, replace, fork, and rewrite software when warranted. Preserve the institution's ability to operate, learn, and maintain useful infrastructure across election cycles.
